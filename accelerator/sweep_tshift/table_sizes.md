# Time-shift ball tables: size and latency

Generated by `accelerator/tablegen/sweep.py` (10000 shots per point, seed 20260903; rotated surface code
memory-X, d rounds, uniform circuit noise p, T = T_max = 2 lattice edge weights, R = 2T).

**Encoding.** A ball entry is `{w, delta = target - source}`. Rows are deduplicated only among
detectors at the same space coordinate, by exact comparison of their `{w, delta}` lists, and
entries with `target <= source` are dropped offline (`prune`). The boundary table carries each
detector's row pointer. *Legacy* is the previous layout: one full ball per detector with absolute
targets. Bits are logical (rows x word width), not padded to a power-of-two SRAM depth.

## Where the cycles go (L = 64, mean per shot)

`fetch` is address-generator scan slots (one per chunk, at least one per defect); `drain` is the
collector's extra cycles for batches with more than one hit. Pruning halves `fetch` but packs the
same edges into fewer batches, so `drain` grows; at high p the collector, not the fetch, bounds
the latency.
